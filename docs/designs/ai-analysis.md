## Problem statement

The scanner is a pure, deterministic pipeline (S0 to S7) over numbers in PostgreSQL. It is honest about what it does
not know: on free data an HK name shows `events.earningsUnknown`, `fundamentals.partialCoverage` ("4y of 10y"),
`iv.proxy`, and the gate can only ask "is the balance sheet sound?" never "is this still a business I would own?".

Milestone 2 bolted six AI research skills onto the Research page (`src/server/research`, prompts from
`<INVEST_AGENT_ROOT>/skills` (a clone of the ai-berkshire prompt repository), run through `claude -p` with read-only tools and a spend cap). They produce good
long-form Markdown reports under `reports/<market>-<symbol>/` and then stop. Nothing downstream reads them:

- the Today list cannot tell that the thesis tracker wrote a red line on a name last week;
- a rights issue found by `news-pulse` never becomes a `corporate_events` row, so S1 cannot FAIL on it;
- an HK results date the model found on HKEXnews does not clear `events.earningsUnknown`;
- a 10-year statement history the model could read out of annual reports does not fill the "4y of 10y" gap;
- `thesis-tracker` always writes version 1.0 because it never sees the previous thesis (TODOS.md);
- an `assignment_risk` alert on an open put arrives on Telegram with no "why did it drop" attached.

The question for this session: where does an LLM make the analysis of the companies and the stocks better, and how
does it do that without breaking the three rules the app is built on (skills are pure, data is point-in-time,
every recommendation is evidence first)?

## What makes this cool

Every morning the shortlist is not just "numbers say sell the 540 put"; next to it sits a dated, cited judgement
about the company written against your own checklists, the next HK results date the app found itself, and the
thesis red lines it is watching. When a put you sold goes in the money, the alert goes out at once and a second
message follows a few minutes later saying whether the drop is noise or a thesis break. And the model never gets
to type a number into the sizing.

## Constraints

- Skills stay pure functions over an as-of-bounded `DataAccess`; no skill may call a model, read the clock or fetch.
- Fundamentals are point-in-time by `reported_at`; anything AI-sourced needs the same discipline (`known_at`).
- Free data only, plus Futu OpenD for HK chains. No new paid feed is assumed.
- The model runs through the existing executor: `claude -p`, read-only tools (WebSearch, WebFetch, Read, the two
  Python helpers), `--max-budget-usd`, `--no-session-persistence`, `--setting-sources project,local`.
- Web content the model reads is untrusted. It must come back as data that validators check, never as instructions.
- The user is on a phone most of the time; outputs must fit the WhatsApp-style UI and a Telegram message.
- The app never places orders. AI output can lower a name's standing (WARN) and can add facts; it cannot size.

## Premises (auto-agreed in autonomous mode, logged in DECISIONS.md)

1. **AI output enters the pipeline only as data rows with provenance** (`source`, `known_at`, citation, confidence),
   through the same writers that call `invalidateSkillRuns`. Skills then read those rows through `DataAccess`
   as-of. Scores stay deterministic and backtestable; a backtest for date D sees only AI rows with `known_at <= D`,
   which for history means none, and the affected rule emits an INFO reason and does not count as a skipped test.
2. **The biggest analysis weaknesses are data gaps, not missing cleverness.** Unknown HK earnings dates, four
   annual statements, manual-only corporate actions. An LLM with web tools is a good extractor for exactly these.
3. **Judgement is stored as a verdict, not a score.** "Would I accept assignment at this strike?" with a confidence
   and red lines is useful to a put seller; a model-produced 0-100 "quality score" blended into the composite is not
   (it cannot be backtested and would drift with the model).
4. **Runs are triggered, bounded and cached**, never per scan. Weekly per watched name, on alert, on a price shock,
   or by hand. Each run has a dollar cap; the day has a cap; every model call goes through `research_runs` so the
   cap is real.
5. **Every AI fact is reviewable and reversible in the UI.** Unconfirmed events and extracted statements show as
   such, can be confirmed or deleted on the Data page, and the scan tags the tier.

Items 1 to 5 are the recommended build. Items 6 to 11 are "Then" (see Next steps); 7 and 8 were moved out of the
first stages on review because they are conveniences, not company analysis, and 7 conflicts with premise 4 unless
it is routed through `research_runs`.

Not recommended:
- A model-generated "moat score" or "management score" blended into `rank.composite`. It cannot be backtested, it
  moves when the model changes, and it hides the evidence. Verdict flags with citations do the same job honestly.
- Letting the model choose strikes or sizes. S4 and S6 stay mechanical; the thesis `max_strike` is a cap the user
  set through their own checklist, applied by S4 as a rule with a Reason.
- Running any AI skill inside `pnpm cli scan`. Scan is database only and must stay fast and offline.

## Approaches considered

### Approach A: Verdict loop (minimal viable)

Items 1, 2, 4. Add a JSON tail to every AI run, store it in `research_verdicts`, give S1 one optional rule and S4
the strike cap, show the chip on Today, send a follow-up "Why" message after alerts, feed the previous thesis
into the tracker.

- Effort: S (CC: about a day). Risk: low. Reuses: research runner, `FakeExecutor` tests, `corporate_events` untouched.
- Pros: the reports finally reach the scanner; the thesis tracker works as designed; alerts get a "why".
- Cons: HK data gaps stay; the user still types HK earnings dates by hand.

### Approach B: AI as a data provider (ideal architecture, recommended)

Approach A plus items 3 and 5 behind one principle: a `src/providers/ai` layer that turns research runs into typed,
validated, provenance-tagged rows through `src/db/writers.ts`, with a confirm queue on the Data page. Skills read
them through `DataAccess` with one additive change to the contract. Staged so each stage ships alone:

- B1 = Approach A.
- B2 = event discovery (item 3) with confirm queue.
- B3 = triggered news attribution and the weekly verdict refresh wired to jobs (item 4 scheduling).
- B4 = statement backfill (item 5) with the accounting-identity validator and two-source agreement.

- Effort: L overall (CC: 3 to 4 days across the stages). Risk: medium, concentrated in B4.
- Pros: closes the HK gaps the whole design has been apologising for; every AI fact is reviewable; backtests stay honest.
- Cons: more tables and UI; B4 needs real care on validation; recurring model spend (bounded, see Cost).

### Approach C: Per-stock analyst agent with memory (lateral)

One long-lived agent per watched name keeps a dossier (thesis, events, filings, news) and the scanner asks the dossier.
Rejected as the primary path: it is Approach B with the data model hidden inside the agent's memory, untestable
with the `FakeExecutor`, and it breaks the as-of rule (the dossier is "now"). The good part, a per-stock dossier
view, is just the Research page reading the rows B produces.

**Recommendation: B, staged, B1 first.** B1 is a one-day change that makes the existing six skills useful to the
scanner; B2 is the single highest-value HK fix; B4 is the only risky stage and ships last, after B2 has proven the
confirm-queue pattern.

## Recommended approach, concretely

### Data model (migration 0008; Drizzle `src/db/schema.ts` is the source of truth)

```
research_verdicts
  id, instrument_id, research_run_id (fk, null only for imported reports), skill_id,
  kind            text  check in ('assignment','thesis','news_attribution','earnings','management','ask')
  as_of           date  not null   -- the run's `today` (the date the judgement was made on)
  known_at        timestamptz not null   -- point-in-time anchor; = run finished_at; imports must supply it
  verdict         text  check in ('accept','accept_with_conditions','reject','unclear')
  confidence      smallint 0..100
  max_strike      numeric null         -- "I would own it at or below this price", trading currency
  red_lines       jsonb  [{ text, metric?, threshold?, breached: bool }]
  thesis_break    text   null check in ('yes','no','unclear')   -- news_attribution only
  summary         text   (<= 400 chars, English, shown on the phone and in Telegram; the report itself may be Chinese)
  citations       jsonb  [{ url, title, date }]
  model, cost_usd
  unique (research_run_id, kind)
corporate_events: no new columns. AI rows use source='ai', confirmed=false,
  details={citation, quote, run_id, kind?}. Privatisation = event_type 'other' with details.kind='privatisation',
  which is what S1 rule 12 already expects.
fundamental_statements: no new columns. AI rows use source='ai_extract', reported_at = announcement date,
  raw={pages:[...], second_source, delta_pct}. The unique key already includes source, so a year Yahoo also has
  would produce two annual rows. B4 change: the history loader (src/db/history.ts) and the valuation dedupe
  (src/valuation/facts.ts, which today keeps the latest reported_at per period_end) both take one row per
  (period_end, period_type) with precedence yahoo > ai_extract, so AI rows only fill years Yahoo lacks.
instruments: add ir_domain text null (company investor-relations host, used by the citation allow-list).
```

`known_at` is what makes a backtest honest: `DataAccess` filters verdicts by `known_at <= knownThrough`, the
same knowledge-time bound events use today (a live scan has `knownThrough` = now and `asOf` = last session).
Contract change (additive): `InstrumentHistory.verdicts`, loaded in `src/db/history.ts`, exposed as
`DataAccess.verdicts()`, and perturbed after the bound in `tests/skills/lookahead.test.ts` like every other table.
Write path: new functions in `src/db/writers.ts` (`writeVerdict`, `writeAiEvents`, `writeAiStatements`) that call
`invalidateSkillRuns` from `as_of - KNOWLEDGE_LOOKBACK_DAYS`, as the event and statement writers already do, so a
cached S1 for the last session is recomputed when a verdict lands today.

### Runner changes (`src/server/research`)

- `prompt.ts`: append rule 7 "End your final message with one fenced block tagged `json` matching this schema
  ..." per skill kind. The schema is in `src/server/research/verdict-schema.ts` (zod, already a dependency) and the
  parser strips the block from the saved Markdown.
- If the tail is missing or invalid: a second, cheap extraction call (`--max-budget-usd 0.2`, no tools) over the
  report text. If that fails too, the run is still `done` with no verdict row and a `research.verdictMissing` note
  on the run. The report is never lost because the JSON is bad.
- `contextFacts()`: add the latest verdict per kind, the previous thesis-tracker report Markdown (read from
  `report_path`) plus its verdict JSON, the next known events, and the open put on the name (strike, expiry, DTE).
  The tracker's "check an existing thesis" mode then works (TODOS item closed).
- Per-skill budget: `OT_SKILL_BUDGET_<id>` overrides `OT_SKILL_MAX_BUDGET_USD` (5) per skill. The light skills
  (events-scout, news-pulse, verdict extraction, thesis-tracker check mode) get a 0.5 default only after one measured
  real run each in B1; until then they run at the global cap. A run that hits its cap comes back from `claude -p`
  as an error and is stored `failed` with reason `budget`; the Settings page counts those, and a job that sees three
  budget failures in a row for one skill stops queueing that skill until the user raises its cap.
- New light skill `events-scout` (prompt in this repo, `src/server/research/prompts/events-scout.md`, not in
  Invest-agent): "find the next results date, any announced rights issue, spin-off, privatisation, AGM or index
  rebalance for X; cite the HKEXnews or IR URL; return JSON". It writes `corporate_events` rows with
  `confirmed=false`.
- New light skill `statements-extract` (B4): one annual period per run, two sources (filing PDF plus
  macrotrends/aastocks per Invest-agent `financial-data.md`), exact decimals via `financial_rigor.py`.
- Jobs: schedules in `src/jobs/scheduler.ts`, kebab-case like the existing ones. `src/jobs` may not import
  `server` (tests/structure.test.ts), so the research jobs are registered as `extra` queues from
  `src/server/index.ts` the way `run-backtest` is, and `JobDeps` gains a `research: { enqueue(symbol, skillId,
  trigger) }` port that the mark job calls; the ResearchManager implements the port.
  `research-weekly` (B3, daily at 02:00 local, so the weekly refresh spreads over the week under the daily cap): for
  every active watched name whose newest `thesis` verdict is older than 7 days, queue thesis-tracker in check mode
  (light) and news-pulse (light); never the medium or heavy skills, those stay manual.
  `research-events` (B2, Sundays 03:00): names with no future earnings event, or none confirmed within 60 days,
  skipping any name that already has an `ai` event with `known_at` in the last 14 days (so an unconfirmed date
  is not re-searched every week while it waits for the user).
  `research-news` (B3, on demand): enqueued by `mark-positions-us/hk` when an alert with code `assignment_risk`,
  `stop2x` or `itm` is raised, and when the day's close moved ±5% (or ±10% over five sessions) on a watched name.
  Env: `OT_AI_DAILY_BUDGET_USD` (default 10) stops the queue for the day when the sum of today's
  `research_runs.cost_usd` reaches it; `OT_AI_AUTO=off` keeps everything manual.
- Alerts are sent synchronously by the mark job and a research run takes minutes (concurrency 2, 40-minute
  timeout), so the alert goes out at once and a second message "Why: ..." follows when the `news_attribution`
  verdict lands. The notifier gets one new message shape for that.

### Validators (the part that keeps the model honest)

- Verdict JSON: zod schema, enum values only, `summary` length, every red line has text, citations are URLs with
  dates. Anything else is rejected and the extraction call runs once.
- Events: date must be a business day in `exchange_calendar` for the market, within the next 400 days, and the
  citation URL must be on an allow-list (hkexnews.hk, sec.gov, the exchange, `instruments.ir_domain`).
  `writeCorporateEvents` updates `source` and `details` of any existing row with the same (type, date), and
  `calendar-refresh` writes `source='yahoo'` rows daily. So `writeAiEvents` skips every same-key row of any source
  (recording "agrees with yahoo/manual" in the run result) and then calls `writeCorporateEvents` without
  `supersede`; and the Yahoo path gets the symmetrical guard (`writeCorporateEvents` option `onlyIfSource`), so
  neither side wipes the other's citation or confirmation. A conflict with a confirmed row on the same type but a
  different date is stored as `details.conflict` on the AI row, not written over.
  Precedence among earnings dates: confirmed (manual or AI) > ai unconfirmed > yahoo unconfirmed > manual
  unconfirmed (the CLI accepts `confirmed=false` on manual rows; the app defaults them to confirmed). Rule 13 and
  `src/db/marks.ts` today take the
  earliest event regardless of `source` and ignore `confirmed`; B2 changes both to pick by that precedence, and
  marks/alerts treat `source='ai', confirmed=false` like the Yahoo estimate they already use (an unconfirmed date
  raises `new_earnings_date` so the user sees it; it does not change sizing).
- Statements (B4): the two sources must agree within 1% on revenue, net income, total assets, CFO; accounting
  identities must hold (assets = liabilities + equity within 0.5%, FCF = CFO - capex); years that overlap Yahoo must
  match Yahoo within 1% (and are then not written, Yahoo wins); otherwise the period is stored in `raw` on a
  rejected-row note in `research_runs.result` and never in the statement row. `reported_at` must be a real
  announcement date with a citation, never estimated; else the row is not written.
- Prompt injection: tool allow-list is already read-only; the JSON tail is the only channel back, and it is
  parsed, not executed. Citations are shown as text, never auto-fetched by the UI.

### Skill changes (pure, versioned; versions are cache keys)

- `S1 fundamental.gate` 1.1.0 -> 1.2.0: rule 14 `thesis.standing`. Reads the latest `assignment` or `thesis` verdict
  with `known_at <= asOf`. `reject` or any `breached` red line: WARN (rule-based, so the 0.85 multiplier applies)
  with the summary as the note. Param `thesisRejectIsFail=false` lets the user make it FAIL. No verdict: an INFO
  reason `thesis.standing = none`, not pushed to `skipped`, and `TOTAL_TESTS` stays 13 so names without a verdict
  (every name in a backtest) do not drift toward `fundamentals.insufficientData`. The payload gains
  `thesisMaxStrike: number | null`.
- `S1` rules 12 and 13: AI events with `confirmed=false` produce `warnData` (data-quality WARN, no multiplier) with
  the citation in the note; confirmed ones behave as manual ones do today. So a wrong unconfirmed date can never FAIL a name.
- `S4 option.select` 1.1.0 -> 1.2.0: reads `thesisMaxStrike` from the S1 payload (it already reads S1 through
  `priorSkillResult`); when `thesisCapsStrike=true` (default) strikes above it are rejected with Reason
  `thesis.strikeAboveComfort` and listed under `rejected`. The earnings window honours unconfirmed AI dates as a WARN
  on the candidate, confirmed as today.
- `S7 explain.narrative` 1.0.0 -> 1.1.0: one line "Thesis (2026-10-05, 72%): accept below 520; red line: ad revenue
  growth < 5%".
- No skill calls a model. Everything above is a read of rows through `DataAccess`.

### UI (phone first)

- Today: a chip per row (`accept 72%`, `reject`, `no thesis`), the verdict summary and red lines in the Reasons tab.
- Research > Reports: the verdict card above the Markdown; a diff against the previous thesis.
- Data: "AI found" queue: unconfirmed events and extracted periods with the citation and Confirm / Delete buttons.
  New routes `PATCH /api/events/:id` (confirm) and `DELETE /api/events/:id`, mirrored in `web/mock`. Confirm sets
  `confirmed=true` (events) or clears the review flag (statements) and invalidates skill runs.
- Positions: the news attribution under the alert; in Telegram as the follow-up message:
  "0700 why: results miss on games (today, 68%); thesis break: unclear. /research/0700".
- Settings: AI spend today and this month (from `research_runs.cost_usd`), auto on/off. A per-skill model picker
  waits for the open question below.

### Cost

Medium and heavy runs cost about US$1 to 5 each today; light runs are capped at US$0.50 once measured. The
automatic work is light only: the weekly refresh of the current 32 watched names (14 HK plus 18 US) is at most
32 x 2 x 0.5 x 4.3 = about US$140 a month as an upper bound, spread over the week by the US$10 daily cap (10 names
a day). Events-scout is a few dollars a month with the 14-day re-run guard. Medium and
heavy skills run only by hand, as now. Every figure is recorded per run in `research_runs.cost_usd`, so the
Settings number is real, not an estimate.

## What I noticed about how you think

- You asked for "AI capabilities and LLM to better analyse the companies and stocks", not "add AI". The design
  answers by putting the model where the analysis is weakest (HK dates, HK statements, the thesis) rather than on top
  of numbers that already work.
- Your docs already said "Narrative text is generated from the reasons, not the other way around" and "No ML
  models; v1 is rule-based and transparent". This design keeps both: the model adds facts and judgements with
  citations, and the rules stay rules.
- You chose seven Invest-agent skills by hand and wrote the "Would I accept assignment?" preset. That preset is the
  verdict kind this design stores first.
